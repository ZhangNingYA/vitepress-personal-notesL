### [cite_start]Why Study Economics [cite: 297]

[cite_start]**1** Economics is the study of how individuals and societies choose to use the scarce resources that nature and previous generations have provided. [cite: 298] [cite_start]The key word in this definition is choose. [cite: 299] [cite_start]Economics is a behavioral, or social, science. [cite: 299] [cite_start]In large measure, it is the study of how people make choices. [cite: 300] [cite_start]The choices that people make, when added up, translate into societal choices. [cite: 301]

[cite_start]**2** Then why study economics? [cite: 302] [cite_start]There are three main reasons to study economics: to learn a way of thinking, to understand society, and to be an informed citizen. [cite: 302]

#### [cite_start]To learn a way of thinking [cite: 303]
[cite_start]**3** Probably the most important reason for studying economics is to learn a way of thinking. [cite: 304] [cite_start]Economics has three fundamental concepts that, once absorbed, can change the way you look at everyday choices: opportunity cost, marginalism, and the working of efficient markets. [cite: 305]

[cite_start]**Opportunity cost** [cite: 306]
[cite_start]**4** What happens in an economy is the outcome of thousands of individual decisions. [cite: 307] [cite_start]People must decide how to divide their incomes among all the goods and services available in the marketplace. [cite: 308] [cite_start]They must decide whether to work, whether to go to school, and how much to save. [cite: 309] [cite_start]Businesses must decide what to produce, how much to produce, how much to charge, and where to locate. [cite: 310] [cite_start]It is not surprising that economic analysis focuses on the process of decision-making. [cite: 311]

[cite_start]**5** Nearly all decisions involve trade-offs. [cite: 312] [cite_start]A key concept that recurs in analyzing the decision-making process is the notion of opportunity cost. [cite: 312] [cite_start]The full "cost" of making a specific choice includes what we give up by not making the best alternative choice. [cite: 313] [cite_start]The best alternative that we forgo, or give up, when we make a choice or a decision, is called the opportunity cost of that decision. [cite: 314]

[cite_start]**6** When asked how much a movie costs, most people cite the ticket price. [cite: 321] [cite_start]For an economist, this is only part of the answer: To see a movie takes not only a ticket but also time. [cite: 322] [cite_start]The opportunity cost of going to a movie is the value of the other things you could have done with the same money and time. [cite: 323] [cite_start]If you decide to take time off from work, the opportunity cost of your leisure is the pay that you would have earned had you worked. [cite: 324] [cite_start]Part of the cost of a college education is the income you could have earned by working full-time instead of going to school. [cite: 325]

[cite_start]**7** Opportunity costs arise because resources are scarce. [cite: 326] [cite_start]Scarce simply means limited. [cite: 326] [cite_start]Consider one of our most important resources — time. [cite: 326, 327] [cite_start]There are only 24 hours in a day, and we must live our lives under this constraint. [cite: 327] [cite_start]A farmer in rural Brazil must decide whether it is better to continue to farm or to go to the city and look for a job. [cite: 328] [cite_start]A hockey player at the University of Vermont must decide whether to play on the varsity team or spend more time studying. [cite: 329]

[cite_start]**Marginalism** [cite: 330]
[cite_start]**8** A second key concept used in analyzing choices is the notion of marginalism. [cite: 331] [cite_start]In weighing the costs and benefits of a decision, it is important to weigh only the costs and benefits that arise from the decision. [cite: 332, 333] [cite_start]Suppose, for example, that you live in New Orleans and that you are weighing the costs and benefits of visiting your mother in Iowa. [cite: 334] [cite_start]If business required that you travel to Kansas City, the cost of visiting Mom would be only the additional, or marginal, time and money cost of getting to Iowa from Kansas City. [cite: 335]

[cite_start]**9** There are numerous examples in which the concept of marginal cost is useful. [cite: 336] [cite_start]For an airplane that is about to take off with empty seats, the marginal cost of an extra passenger is essentially zero; [cite: 337] [cite_start]the total cost of the trip is roughly unchanged by the addition of an extra passenger. [cite: 338] [cite_start]Thus, setting aside a few seats to be sold at big discounts through www.priceline.com or other websites can be profitable even if the fare for those seats is far below the average cost per seat of making the trip. [cite: 339] [cite_start]As long as the airline succeeds in filling seats that would otherwise have been empty, doing so is profitable. [cite: 340]

[cite_start]**Efficient markets No free lunch** [cite: 341]
[cite_start]**10** Suppose you are ready to check out of a busy grocery store on the day before a storm and seven checkout registers are open with several people in each line. [cite: 342] [cite_start]Which line should you choose? [cite: 349] [cite_start]Usually, the waiting time is approximately the same no matter which register you choose (assuming you have more than 12 items). [cite: 349] [cite_start]If one line is much shorter than the others, people will quickly move into it until the lines are equalized again. [cite: 350]

[cite_start]**11** The term profit in economics has a very precise meaning. [cite: 351] [cite_start]Economists, however, often loosely refer to "good deals" or risk-free ventures as profit opportunities. [cite: 352] [cite_start]Using the term loosely, a profit opportunity exists at the checkout lines when one line is shorter than the others. [cite: 353] [cite_start]In general, such profit opportunities are rare. [cite: 354] [cite_start]At any time, many people are searching for them; as a consequence, few exist. [cite: 354, 355] [cite_start]Markets like this, where any profit opportunities are eliminated almost instantaneously, are said to be efficient markets. [cite: 355]

[cite_start]**12** The common way of expressing the efficient markets concept is "there is no such thing as a free lunch." [cite: 356] [cite_start]How should you react when a stockbroker calls with a hot tip on the stock market? [cite: 357] [cite_start]With skepticism. [cite: 357] [cite_start]Thousands of individuals each day are looking for hot tips in the market. [cite: 358] [cite_start]If a particular tip about a stock is valid, there will be an immediate rush to buy the stock, which will quickly drive up its price. [cite: 359] [cite_start]This view that very few profit opportunities exist can, of course, be carried too far. [cite: 360] [cite_start]There is a story about two people walking along, one an economist and one not. [cite: 361] [cite_start]The non-economist sees a $20 bill on the sidewalk and says, "There's a $20 bill on the sidewalk." [cite: 362] [cite_start]The economist replies, "That is not possible. If there were, somebody would already have picked it up." [cite: 363]

[cite_start]**13** There are clearly times when profit opportunities exist. [cite: 364] [cite_start]Someone has to be first to get the news, and some people have quicker insights than others. [cite: 364] [cite_start]Nevertheless, news travels fast, and there are thousands of people with quick insights. [cite: 365] [cite_start]The general view that large profit opportunities are rare is close to the mark. [cite: 366]

#### [cite_start]To understand society [cite: 367]
[cite_start]**14** Another reason for studying economics is to understand society better. [cite: 368] [cite_start]Past and present economic decisions have an enormous influence on the character of life in a society. [cite: 369] [cite_start]The current state of the physical environment, the level of material well-being, and the nature and number of jobs are all products of the economic system. [cite: 370]

[cite_start]**15** At no time has the impact of economic change on a society been more evident than in England during the late eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries, a period that we now call the Industrial Revolution. [cite: 371] [cite_start]Increases in the productivity of agriculture, new manufacturing technologies, and development of more efficient forms of transportation led to a massive movement of the British population from the countryside to the city. [cite: 372, 377] [cite_start]At the beginning of the eighteenth century, approximately 2 out of 3 people in Great Britain worked in agriculture. [cite: 377] [cite_start]By 1812, only 1 in 3 remained in agriculture; by 1900, the figure was fewer than 1 in 10. [cite: 378] [cite_start]People jammed into overcrowded cities and worked long hours in factories. [cite: 378] [cite_start]England had changed completely in two centuries — a period that in the run of history was nothing more than the blink of an eye. [cite: 379, 380]

[cite_start]**16** It is not surprising that the discipline of economics began to take shape during this period. [cite: 381] [cite_start]Social critics and philosophers looked around and knew that their philosophies must expand to accommodate the changes. [cite: 382] [cite_start]Adam Smith's The Wealth of Nations appeared in 1776. [cite: 383] [cite_start]It was followed by the writings of David Ricardo, Karl Marx, Thomas Malthus, and others. [cite: 383] [cite_start]Each tried to make sense out of what was happening. [cite: 384] Who was building the factories? [cite_start]Why? [cite: 384] [cite_start]What determined the level of wages paid to workers or the price of food? [cite: 385] [cite_start]What would happen in the future, and what should happen? [cite: 386] [cite_start]The people who asked these questions were the first economists. [cite: 386, 387] [cite_start]The study of economics is an essential part of the study of society. [cite: 387]

#### [cite_start]To be an informed citizen [cite: 388]
[cite_start]**17** A knowledge of economics is essential to being an informed citizen. [cite: 388] [cite_start]Between 2009 and 2013 much of the world struggled with a major recession and very slow recovery, leaving millions of people around the world out of work. [cite: 389] [cite_start]Understanding what happens in a recession and what the government can and cannot do to help in a recovery is an essential part of being an informed citizen. [cite: 390]

[cite_start]**18** Economics is also essential in understanding a range of other everyday government decisions at the local and federal levels. [cite: 391] [cite_start]Why do governments pay for public schools and roads, but not cell phones? [cite: 392] [cite_start]In 2010, the federal government moved toward universal health care for US citizens. [cite: 393] [cite_start]How do you understand the debate of whether this is or is not a good idea? [cite: 394] [cite_start]Every day, across the globe, people engage in political decision-making around questions like these, questions that depend on an understanding of economics. [cite: 395]
